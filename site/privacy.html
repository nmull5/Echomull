EchoMull Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 29, 2026

EchoMull is a free iPhone app that shows live captions of the conversations around you as colored chat bubbles. This page explains, in plain words, what happens to your information.

Short version

  • What people say stays on your iPhone. Speech recognition, speaker colors, saved voices, sound alerts, translation and spoken replies all work on the phone. The app never sends audio anywhere. We have no servers, and we never receive, store or share your audio, captions, typed replies, saved voices or your name.
  • No ads, no account, no tracking. EchoMull doesn't collect any data about you. It has no ads, no analytics and no crash-reporting code, and no ad or tracking company gets data through the app. (If you've turned on Share With App Developers in iPhone Settings, Apple may send the developer anonymous crash reports.)
  • The app uses the internet only to download files it needs: Apple's speech and translation files, and the speaker-color files from Hugging Face. These are plain downloads. No audio, captions or personal information is sent.

What stays on your iPhone

Information the app keeps, where it lives and how to remove it
Information Where it lives How to remove it
Microphone audio Used in memory while captions are on (for captions, speaker colors, sound alerts and "how it was said"), then thrown away. It's never saved to a file, except when you save a voice (next row). Nothing is kept.
Saved voices (optional) Only if you choose to save one. Up to about 12 seconds of that person talking, plus the name and color you picked. Kept inside the app and left out of iCloud and computer backups. Settings > Saved voices, tap the voice, then Delete voice. Or delete the app.
Captions, speaker names and colors Saved inside the app, only if Settings > Save conversations is on (it's on at first). Replies you type and add to a conversation, sound alerts shown in the conversation (for example "Doorbell · 14:05"), "(laughing)"-style tags and translations are saved the same way, as part of the conversation. Delete a conversation in History, use Settings > Delete all conversations, or delete the app.
Your name and nicknames (optional) Only if you type them for the "Someone says my name" alert. Stored with your settings and used only to look for them in the captions on your phone. Clear the name box in Settings > Sound alerts, Settings > Reset all settings, or delete the app.
Settings (text size, Near and Far, language, sound alerts, voice, translation language and so on) Stored inside the app. Settings > Reset all settings, or delete the app.
Saved PDFs Made on the phone when you tap Save PDF. A copy is kept in the app's temporary files until iOS clears them. Delete the app, or let iOS clear its temporary files. Copies you saved or sent elsewhere are yours to delete there.
Diagnostics log Kept in memory while the app runs. Shown only if you open Settings > Diagnostics, and sent only if you copy it and send it yourself. Closing the app clears it.

Captions, settings and the names of saved voices can be part of your own iCloud or computer backups, like other app data. Those backups are controlled by you and Apple, not by us. Saved voice recordings are never backed up, so on a new phone you record them again.

When you use Share or Save PDF in History, you choose where the conversation goes (for example Files, AirDrop, Messages or Mail). What happens after that is up to the app or person you share with.

Saved voices

  • Only when you choose. The app records a voice only when you tap Save my voice or Add a voice and read three short lines. It never saves voices on its own.
  • Ask first. Before you save someone else's voice, ask them. The app won't start until you tick "[Name] said it's OK to save their voice."
  • On this phone only. The recording stays inside the app on your iPhone. It's used only to recognize that person in your conversations. It's never uploaded, never sent to us, Google, Apple or anyone else, and never included in backups.
  • Yours to change. Rename it, record it again, stop recognizing it, or delete it at any time in Settings > Saved voices. Deleting a voice doesn't change past conversations in History; they keep the names they had.

Sound alerts, spoken replies and translation

  • Sound alerts (doorbell, smoke alarm, "someone said my name" and so on) use Apple's sound recognizer that is built into iOS. It listens to the same microphone audio as the captions, on the phone, only while EchoMull is listening. No audio is recorded or sent anywhere. Your name is matched against the captions on the phone. Sound alerts don't replace smoke alarms or other safety devices.
  • "How things are said" ((laughing), (shouting), louder words in bold) is worked out on the phone from the same audio.
  • Spoken replies use the voices built into your iPhone (Apple's speech voices). The words you type are turned into speech on the phone and aren't sent anywhere. If you choose Use my Personal Voice, iOS asks whether EchoMull may use your Personal Voice. EchoMull only uses it to speak your replies out loud on your phone. You can take back that permission in the iPhone's Settings > Accessibility > Personal Voice.
  • Translation uses Apple's Translation feature on the phone (iOS 18 or newer). Captions are translated on the iPhone, not sent to a server.

Network connections

EchoMull has no servers of its own, no ads, no analytics and no account. The iPhone app connects to the internet only for the two downloads below.

1. Apple speech and translation files

On first use, when you pick a new language, and when you turn on Voices that are hard to catch (iOS 26 or later), iOS may download speech files from Apple. If you turn on translation, iOS may ask to download the translation languages you picked. If you download extra voices in the iPhone's Settings, iOS downloads those too. iOS handles these downloads under Apple's Privacy Policy. The app asks for speech recognition and translation on the phone itself, so your speech and captions aren't sent to Apple to be turned into text or translated.

2. Speaker-color files from Hugging Face

To color bubbles by speaker, the app downloads a speaker file once (about 100 MB, or 250 MB for the higher-accuracy option) from Hugging Face, a public file-hosting service. It's an ordinary download: like any website, Hugging Face can see your IP address and basic request details. No audio, captions, voices or personal information are sent. See the Hugging Face Privacy Policy.

That's all. The app doesn't show Apple's "Allow tracking" prompt because it doesn't track you, and it never reads your advertising identifier (IDFA).

Permissions

  • Microphone: needed to hear the people around you, to listen for sounds and to save a voice. Audio stays on the iPhone. If you turn on Keep listening in the background, it keeps listening while the phone is locked, until you tap Pause.
  • Speech Recognition: on some iPhones and iOS versions, iOS asks for this before captions can work.
  • Personal Voice (optional): asked only if you tap Use my Personal Voice.
  • Notifications (optional): asked only if you turn on Keep listening in the background. Alerts are made on the phone. They never show caption text, and nothing goes through a server.

The app explains why before iOS asks for the microphone. If you said no, the app shows the exact steps and an Open Settings button so you can turn it back on. You can turn these off at any time in the iPhone's Settings app. The app can't caption without the microphone.

Browser test version

The developer also uses a version of EchoMull that runs in a web browser, only to test the app on a computer. It's a developer tool, not the iPhone app, and it works differently:

  • In Chrome and Edge, the browser's own speech recognition sends microphone audio to Google or Microsoft to turn it into text, under their privacy policies. The iPhone app never does this.
  • For speaker colors, the browser downloads the transformers.js library from jsDelivr and a speaker file from Hugging Face. As with any download, those services can see the computer's IP address and basic request details. The audio used for speaker colors stays inside the browser and isn't uploaded.
  • For sound alerts and "how things are said", the browser downloads a small sound helper (about 11 MB) once: Google's MediaPipe code from jsDelivr and Google's YAMNet sound file from Google's file storage. Those services can see the computer's IP address and basic request details. The helper then runs inside the browser; the audio is not uploaded. The helper's usage reports to Google are blocked.
  • Translation uses the translator built into Chrome or Edge on the computer. The browser may download its translation languages from Google or Microsoft the first time; the captions are translated on the computer.
  • Spoken replies use the browser's voices. Voices marked Online in the voice list send the words of your reply to Google or Microsoft to be spoken. The other voices work on the computer.
  • Save PDF opens the browser's print window. The PDF is made on the computer and saved where you choose.
  • Saved voices in the browser are stored only in that browser on that computer (its own storage, IndexedDB), together with a list of numbers that describes each voice. Recording a voice uses the microphone directly, not Google's or Microsoft's speech recognition, and nothing is uploaded. Delete them in Settings > Saved voices, or by clearing the site's data in the browser.
  • Optional test recordings (audio and captions) are stored only in that browser on that computer until the person testing deletes or exports them. Record only people who have agreed to it.

Children

EchoMull is not directed at children under 13. It doesn't collect personal information from anyone, children included.

Your choices

  • Turn off Save conversations so that nothing is written to History.
  • Turn off Sound alerts, Show how things are said or Translate captions in Settings at any time.
  • Delete conversations, saved voices or all settings at any time (see the table above).
  • Deleting the app removes everything it stored on your iPhone.

Because we don't collect or store your personal information on any server, there is nothing we hold that we could send to you or delete for you.
